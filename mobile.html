Coupler-Curve Thermometer

A design simulator for a garden sculpture that shows temperature. An actuator drives a bell crank into two chained four-bar linkages; the indicator is the second stage's coupler point, and the path it traces is the scale you engrave. Equal temperature steps land at unequal spacing — that is the whole idea.

Shaping it — by hand, not by slider

This build has no geometry sliders. Every length and mount position is set by dragging its pin on the drawing, and the values you are changing appear at the top of the screen while you drag.

Drag a mount or pin
Reshapes the linkage. The sweep keeps running and winds up to full speed while you drag, so the full excursion of your change plays out under your finger; let go and it returns to your speed setting. The four large dots are the fixed mounts; the smaller ones are the moving joints, and those move with the animation — pause with ⏸ if you want one to hold still.
Pinch
Zoom. This is also how you get fine control: a drag works in real inches, so zoomed right in one pixel is about a hundredth of an inch.
One finger on empty space
Pan. Two fingers pan as well.
Double-tap
Recentre and reset zoom — same as the ⌖ button.
Tick the Dimensions box
Labels every link, both plates and each mount's x, y, all measured from the current pose.

Need to type an exact number instead? The desktop page keeps the full slider panel.

The buttons

▶ / ⏸
Play or pause the temperature sweep. Pausing resumes from the temperature on screen, so it never jumps.
↶
Undo — drags, presets, Reset, loading a design. 60 deep, and greyed out when there is nothing to undo.
⟲
Reset everything to how the page opened — geometry, every setting, rotation, zoom and pan. One ↶ puts it all back.
⌖
Recentre and reset zoom. Leaves the rotation alone.

The grey bar
Tap or drag it to open and close the control sheet.

Reading the path

A black division crosses the path every 1 °F, labelled every 10°. Where they bunch, the indicator barely moves; where they spread, it races. The indicator is a transparent ring so you read the divisions through it — on the real piece it will be a ring with a hole. Labels thin out where they would overlap; zoom in and the rest reappear.

The sheet

Preset
Serpentine is the chosen design. Any edit switches to "custom"; Reset preset restores it and squares up the view.
Temperature
Scale min/max, the reading, Animate and its speed. Reverse scale makes the retracted end hot instead of cold.
Display
Ticks, inner curves (every joint's swept trace), the bounding box, dimensions, and view rotation in 10° steps.
Bounding box
The footprint of everything — path, swept joints and all four mounts — at the current view angle.
Actuator drive
The excursion — the slice of the 18″ stroke actually used — and the two assembly-branch flips. Crank radius and anchor position are set by dragging R and the anchor mount.

Saving and exporting

Type a name and press Save to keep a design in this browser; recall it from the dropdown. The page always opens on its built-in defaults — nothing is restored automatically.

Parts DXF writes the five links laid flat, one layer each. Points DXF writes the path, its divisions, the labels and the mounts. Both are in inches, ready for Fusion.
